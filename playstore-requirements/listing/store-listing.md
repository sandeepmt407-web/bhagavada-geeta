## Full description
*(max 4000 characters — this is about 2,150)*

```
The complete Bhagavad Gita — all eighteen chapters and every one of its 701 verses — set over a real-time 3D Kurukshetra that changes with what you are reading.

THE TEXT
Every verse carries its Sanskrit in Devanagari, an IAST transliteration, and a word-by-word gloss, alongside a translation you choose. Eight translations across three languages are included: five in English, two in Hindi, and one in French.

Swami Sivananda · Shri Purohit Swami · Swami Gambirananda · Swami Adidevananda · Dr. S. Sankaranarayan · Swami Tejomayananda · Swami Ramsukhdas · Émile Senart

Switch between them on any verse. The Sanskrit never changes — only the reading of it does.

A SCENE THAT FOLLOWS THE ARGUMENT
The chariot halted between two armies is drawn in real time, not painted once. Ten different treatments of light, air and sky follow the movement of the text: cold before first light for Arjuna's despair, a blaze of gold for the vision of the universal form in the eleventh chapter, a still starry night for the sixth chapter's lamp in a windless place. Every verse shifts the sky and the framing a little further, so no two pages are quite the same image.

READ ALOUD
Verses are narrated by your phone's own speech engine, in the language of whatever you are reading — a Hindi voice for the Hindi translation, not an English one mispronouncing it. Choose the shloka or its meaning and every verse reads that as it opens. Pick whichever voice on your device you like best. A bansuri plays quietly underneath and steps out of the way whenever a verse is being read.

BUILT FOR ACTUALLY READING IT
· Four text sizes, with a real line of verse shown at each one
· Search across every translation at once
· Bookmarks, and reading progress across all 701 verses
· Eight themed collections for when you do not want to start at 1.1
· A verse each morning, if you want one
· Share any verse as an image

NO ACCOUNT, NO ADVERTS, NO TRACKING
There is nothing to sign in to. There are no adverts, no analytics, and no third-party tracking of any kind. Your bookmarks, progress and settings stay on your phone and are never sent anywhere. The entire scripture is inside the download and the app works offline.

A note on narration: Android provides no Sanskrit voice, so when the app reads the shloka it uses a Hindi one, which is the only voice able to pronounce the script. It is an aid to following the text rather than a model for recitation.
```

## Data safety form

The Play Console will ask you to declare this. Based on what the app actually does:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **No** |
| Is all of the user data encrypted in transit? | Not applicable — the app transmits no user data |
| Do you provide a way for users to request that their data is deleted? | Not applicable — uninstalling removes everything |

**One thing to be careful and honest about.** The app itself collects nothing, but when
narration uses an online voice, the *device's* speech engine sends the verse text to its
provider to be synthesised. That is not user data and it is not collected by you — it is
the same thing that happens whenever any app uses network text-to-speech — but the
privacy policy discloses it plainly, and you should not claim in the listing that nothing
ever leaves the device. Users who want that can pick an offline voice in the app.
