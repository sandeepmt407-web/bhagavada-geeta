3. Translations are the translators' own

A translation is an interpretation. The readings offered here differ from one another — sometimes considerably — because the translators differed. Nothing in the app is put forward as the single correct meaning of a verse, and the app takes no position on matters of doctrine.

4. Narration is synthesised

Verses are read aloud by the text-to-speech engine on your own phone, not from recordings made for this app. The quality, accent and pronunciation are your device's, and they vary between phones.

This matters for the Sanskrit in particular. Android provides no Sanskrit voice, so when the app is set to read the shloka it uses a Hindi voice, that being the only voice able to pronounce the script at all. Hindi pronunciation rules are not Sanskrit's, and the result is an approximation, not correct recitation. It is offered as an aid to following the text, and it should not be relied on as a model for recitation or for learning pronunciation.
